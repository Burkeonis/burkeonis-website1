MASTERED RECORD / DEEP ANALYSIS

LIFE
HAPPENS.

This song is not about one tragedy. It is not about addiction, losing my children, failed relationships, or even regret.

It is about accepting one unavoidable truth: life does not negotiate. There is almost no blame, no rage, no villain, and no enemy. Just reality.

THE TRUTH
Suffering is not always punishment. Sometimes it is simply the price of being alive.
01 / THE TITLE

NO ONE GETS TO SKIP THIS.

People often say “life happens” to minimize pain. I use it to explain pain. Life eventually reaches everyone—not because they are bad, and not because they deserve it, but because they are alive.

The words sound casual. What sits underneath them is enormous.

02 / LIVING GRIEF

CLOSE ENOUGH TO SEE. TOO FAR TO REACH.

I have three boys still walking this earth, but they feel farther away than the moon. This is not death. In some ways it is harder because hope still exists, which means the grief never finishes.

Death creates finality. Distance creates waiting. Every birthday becomes another reminder. Every year becomes another unanswered question.

Some people lose people to death. Some people lose them while they are alive.

Society understands funerals. It does not understand watching someone exist without being part of their life. There is no ceremony and no closure—only waiting.

03 / MEMORY AS ACCOUNTABILITY

THE PAST KEEPS KNOCKING.

I know every birthday I missed and every empty room. Memory has become accountability. I am not pretending those years did not happen. I carry them because I refuse to forget.

I do not need another lecture. That is not defensiveness; it is exhaustion. I have already replayed every mistake. The harshest judge has already been me.

Every bad decision still shows up at my door. That is how unresolved regret works. It does not stay in the past. It knocks again and again.

04 / LIFE DOES NOT ASK PERMISSION

LOSS ARRIVES WITHOUT AN APPOINTMENT.

We prepare for birthdays, vacations, and careers. We never prepare for the moments that change everything because loss does not wait until we are stronger.

It takes the things you would die to keep—and leaves you standing there.

Maybe it is not fair. Maybe it was never meant to be. But value exists because things can end. If nothing could be lost, nothing would feel precious. Impermanence gives meaning.

05 / THE WOUND AND THE KNIFE

I REFUSE TO REMOVE MYSELF FROM THE STORY.

I have left damage in my wake. No excuses, no blaming addiction, childhood, or anyone else. Just ownership.

I have been both the wound and the knife.

Sometimes we are victims. Sometimes we are the ones causing pain. Most people admit only one. Growth begins when I stop asking only who is to blame and start asking what part I played.

Sometimes the villain in my story looked exactly like my eyes—not because I am evil, but because I refuse to erase myself from the consequences of my life.

06 / THE MIRROR

SELF-AWARENESS IS NOT ALWAYS COMFORTING.

Regret is a heavy suitcase. It was built to be carried, but dragging it forever will never rewrite yesterday. If it cannot change the past, why let it decide the future?

The mirror knows what I do not tell anyone else. It never argues, flatters, or lies. It simply reflects. Some nights I know the man in it too well.

07 / NOBODY GETS THROUGH UNTOUCHED

SCARS ARE NOT PROOF I AM BROKEN.

I used to believe the lesson was never to break. That was youth speaking. I thought strength meant invincibility. Then life happened.

Now I know nobody gets through untouched. Strength is not avoiding scars. It is continuing despite them. Scars are evidence of surviving, loving, and paying the emotional price for caring.

I know tomorrow may take another piece. That is not hopelessness. It is acceptance without surrender. Loss will happen again, and I will keep going.

08 / THE FATHER WHO WAITS

THE RELATIONSHIP IS UNFINISHED IN MY HEART.

A phone call. A goodbye. A closed door. A memory. Life-changing moments often arrive quietly, with no warning and no dramatic music.

Three boys somewhere living life—and a father learning how to wait.

I do not say a father giving up. I say a father learning how to wait. Waiting hurts because hope still exists.

Life hurts because it mattered. Pain is evidence. The opposite of love is not pain. It is indifference.

09 / WHAT IT MEANS NOW

ACCEPTING REALITY WITHOUT SURRENDERING HOPE.

I was not trying to write a sad song. I was trying to make peace with reality. I had stopped asking “Why me?” and stopped looking for someone to blame. I was learning to carry grief without letting it consume me.

We all leave wounds. We all carry wounds. We all lose people. We all become someone we never imagined. The question is not whether life breaks us. It is what we choose to become afterward.

This is one of the defining songs of Burkeonis—not because it is the darkest, but because it is the wisest. The father is still waiting. The man still owns his mistakes. The scars still exist. None of them become an excuse to stop living.

The song does not promise healing.

It does not promise justice.

TOMORROW WILL STILL COME.

And somehow—so will you.
